About

My dad handed me this at 18.

Portrait photo

My dad introduced me to biohacking when I was 18. I've been experimenting on myself ever since. Some of it was smart, and some of it I'd do differently now.

Then came three knee surgeries and a foot surgery. They took away the training I loved. My mental health took a hit too, and I spent years in what I call the gap: lost, without the clear path everyone around me seemed to have, and going deep into the dark looking for what was true. I don't see those years as wasted anymore. They were my body refusing to settle, and they taught me most of what I know.

I didn't find my way back in a gym. I found it outside: getting up for the sunrise, eating with the sun, breathing, getting cold, rolling on the jiu-jitsu mats and swinging a rope. Most of what helped me comes down to biology and physics: light, food, the nervous system, movement and what I let into my body and my house.

The inner work

The body was only half of it. Just as much of my way back came from inner work. Those dark years left me with something I wouldn't trade: depth. When you don't fit the status quo, you start questioning everything, and I never stopped. I've spent years studying mystical and esoteric teachings, looking for what's actually true instead of what I was told.

A big part of that has been working with a mentor, a mystic with great knowledge, depth, life experience and wisdom. He helped me regain my power and keep my essence instead of trading it to fit in.

What I've come to believe is that you already have everything you need within you. The science gives me the how. The inner work gives me the why. I'm still seeking, and this site is part of that.

Now I live with and help care for my 85-year-old grandmother, and I've used the same ideas to set up her home.

What this site is

It's a public notebook. The rhythms are what I do. The experiments are what I'm testing. The Sunrise Log is proof I actually show up.

What it isn't

Medical advice. I'm sharing my own experience, and I tag everything by how strong the evidence is. If you're dealing with mental-health struggles, please talk to a professional. What helped me worked alongside real support, not instead of it.
