The Why

Why I do what I do.

Everything I do comes from one idea: your body runs on the environment you put it in. The person who's shaped that idea for me more than anyone is Dr. Jack Kruse. A lot of his model is still hypothesis, so I label every piece. You'll see the idea, where it comes from, and how sure anyone actually is.

The big idea: inputs first

Kruse sees a person as an energy system plugged into nature. Light, darkness, cold, water and food come first. Hormones, hunger, mood and repair come after, as the body's answer to those inputs. Change the inputs and the rest follows. That's the hypothesis.

The thread that ties it together: leptin

Leptin is a hormone your fat makes. It tells your brain how much energy you have stored. Your brain answers through what's called the melanocortin system:

  1. FATLeptin

    "Here's how much fuel we have"

  2. BRAINPOMC

    Leptin switches these neurons on

  3. SIGNALα-MSH

    POMC gets cut into this messenger

  4. RECEPTORMC4R

    Hunger goes down, energy use goes up

Kruse's twist: your skin and eyes make POMC too, and sunlight switches it on. So in his model, the sun feeds the same circuit your fat does. Live indoors all day under bright screens at night and the signal gets scrambled. The brain stops hearing leptin. That's leptin resistance: you're storing fat while your body acts like it's starving.

● Strong: the brain pathway itself, and UV making POMC in skin (that's how you tan). A broken MC4R is the most common single-gene cause of obesity. ◇ Hypothesis: that sunlight runs your appetite and weight through this pathway in any big way.

Sources: Zhang Y et al. (1994), the discovery of leptin, Nature. Farooqi IS et al. (2003), MC4R mutations and obesity, NEJM. Cui R et al. (2007), UV, p53 and POMC in the tanning response, Cell. Kruse: Leptin: Chapter One; QT #22: Leptin Resistance is Melanopsin Dysfunction (public intro, rest is paid).

LIGHT● Strong◇ Hypothesis

Morning light

What I do

I eat about an hour before sunrise, then I'm outside about 40 minutes before the sun comes up. No sunglasses, no windshield, phone in my pocket. I stay for the whole dawn.

Kruse's hypothesis

Your eye is a clock as well as a camera. The retina sends timing to the master clock in your brain, and that clock sets every tissue. It's not only about how bright the light is: the order of colors at an outdoor dawn tells your body what time and what season it is. A lamp can't do that.

What research shows

● Strong: morning light moves your body clock earlier and makes sleep easier. ◇ Hypothesis: that the dawn sequence of colors matters beyond plain brightness. Note: dawn isn't a UVB window. UV shows up once the sun is higher. Never look straight at the sun.

Sources: Khalsa SBS et al. (2003), phase response curve to bright light, J Physiol. Wright KP Jr et al. (2013), natural light-dark cycle and the human clock, Current Biology. Kruse: Time #1: Is our retina our metronome?; Time #17.

Eating with the sun

What I do

Real food early, before coffee and before the phone. Eggs, meat, fish, fruit, whatever I have. Breakfast is about an hour before sunrise.

Kruse's hypothesis

His original Leptin Rx: a big protein breakfast soon after waking, three meals, no snacking, nothing for about four hours before bed. The goal is to give leptin clear, predictable signals so your brain can hear it again. Later he moved further upstream: food can't fix a body living under the wrong light. And fasting comes after the reset, not instead of it.

What research shows

◐ Emerging: in a small controlled trial, eating earlier in the day improved insulin sensitivity and blood pressure, even with no weight loss. ◇ Hypothesis: that a protein breakfast "resets" leptin specifically. Nobody has tested that directly.

Sources: Sutton EF et al. (2018), early time-restricted feeding, Cell Metabolism. Kruse: My Leptin Prescription; The Leptin Rx: FAQs.

LIGHT● Strong◇ Hypothesis

Dark evenings

What I do

Bright days, dim evenings. That's how I set up my grandmother's house, and it's the last day of the Reset: catch the sunset, then low lights and dim screens until bed.

Kruse's hypothesis

Night is the repair shift. Darkness lets melatonin rise, and in his model melatonin works inside your mitochondria, not only in your brain. Bright artificial light after dark, especially blue-heavy screens and LEDs, tells your body it's still midday, so the repair shift gets cut short. A melatonin pill isn't the same as making your own.

What research shows

● Strong: ordinary room light before bed suppresses and delays your melatonin. ◇ Hypothesis: his bigger claims, like indoor blue light being harmful during the day too, or doing lasting damage to cells.

Sources: Gooley JJ et al. (2011), room light before bedtime suppresses melatonin, J Clin Endocrinol Metab. Kruse: Time #12; QT #10: Why daytime indoor blue light is toxic (public intro, rest is paid).

Getting cold

What I do

A 10-minute face plunge in water at 55°F or colder. I breathe through a snorkel and hum as I breathe out through my nose. Then a cold shower, and another face plunge at bedtime.

Kruse's hypothesis

He calls it cold thermogenesis: an ancient program that runs through the same leptin-melanocortin pathway. Cold tells your brain it's winter, switches on brown fat and shifts you toward burning fat. He thinks it can help the brain hear leptin again. He's also clear that it's optional, and that easing into cold is a different thing from extreme cold.

What research shows

● Strong: cold switches on brown fat in adults. ◇ Hypothesis: that cold restores leptin sensitivity. ○ n=1: the snorkel and the humming are my own addition.

Sources: van Marken Lichtenbelt WD et al. (2009), cold-activated brown fat in healthy men, NEJM. Kruse: Cold Thermogenesis 6: The Ancient Pathway; Cold Thermogenesis 13: The FAQs.

Be careful with cold

Cold water can cause cold shock, fainting and heart trouble. Start mild, never get in alone, and get warm right away if your shivering stops while you're still cold. Talk to your doctor first if you have a heart condition.

Parts of the map I'm still reading

Kruse goes much further than this. These are ideas I'm studying, not things I claim or tell you to do. Almost all of them are hypothesis, and some sit behind his paywall, so I've only read how they start.

  • Seafood and DHA

    DHA is the fat your retina and brain are built from. Kruse treats it as the hardware that receives light, which is why he wants whole seafood over fish oil. ◇

  • Water as structure

    Water next to cell membranes forming an ordered, charged layer ("EZ water") that stores energy. ◇

  • Deuterium

    The heavy form of hydrogen, and how your mitochondria sort it. Not "all deuterium is bad": timing and place matter in his model. ◇

  • Man-made fields

    Wi-Fi, cell signals and wiring as inputs that throw off timing. His most contested idea. ◇

  • The human part

    Dopamine, choices and relationships feeding back into your energy. The conditions you live in shape your choices, and your choices shape the conditions. ◇

Here's how I hold all of it. I live the parts that are cheap, safe and free: sun, dark, real food, cold water. I don't need the whole theory to be true for those to be worth doing. And I keep asking questions.
